What it does

One place for content, schedules and every screen

The short version. The home page has the full list.

Content and playlists

Upload pictures, video, PDFs and presentations in the format you have. The Console converts them for each screen.

Layouts and schedules

Split a screen into zones, and set what plays by hour, day and date, for one screen, a group or a whole site.

Plays on offline

Screens keep to their schedule for weeks if the connection drops.

Emergency messages

One message over every screen in the organisation, or at the sites you choose, until someone clears it.

Proof of play

A record of what each screen showed, when and for how long.

Pro

Approval, video walls and your own tools

Have changes wait for an approver, join screens into one picture, drive the Console with API tokens, and connect Home Assistant.

Security and your data

How the Console keeps what you give it

What your IT team will ask first, in plain words.

Screens connect out

Each screen connects out to the Console on port 443 and signs in with its own key. No port is opened at a site.

Each organisation kept apart

Each organisation's data is stored apart, with its own encryption key. Nothing is shared between organisations.

Secrets and passwords

Secrets you give the Console, such as a single sign-on client secret or a saved sign-in, are encrypted with your organisation's key. Passwords are kept only as hashes. The Console keeps no card details.

A tamper-evident activity log

Every sign-in, change and command is in your activity log. Each entry is chained to the one before, so an entry edited or removed shows. You choose how long it is kept, from 30 days to 10 years.

Records you can take and check

Owners can export the organisation's setup and its activity log. Proof of play is chained too, and anyone with an export can check it. On Enterprise, the activity log streams to your SIEM as it is written.

Support access is yours to open

Our support staff sign in to your organisation only while one of your owners has opened support access, for a set time, as a viewer or an operator. It closes by itself, and it is in your activity log.

Sign-in and access control

Your sign-in, your roles, your rules

Use the Console's own sign-in with two-factor, or the identity provider you already run.

Signing in

Owners set the rules for their organisation. Where a line names a plan, it starts at that plan.

  • Two-factor sign-in with a passkey, an authenticator app, or both
  • Owners can require two-factor sign-in for every member
  • Password rules your owners set: length, age and no reuse
  • Sessions that end by themselves, with limits your owners can shorten
  • Single sign-on with OpenID Connect Pro
  • Single sign-on with SAML 2.0 Enterprise
  • SCIM, so your directory adds and removes people Enterprise
  • Access only from the networks you list Enterprise

Who may do what

Each person has a role in the organisation. The roles are made for common jobs, so nobody gets more than their work needs.

  • Owner: members, sites and everything below
  • Operator: screens, schedules, emergency messages and content
  • Content manager: content and schedules, and approves others' changes
  • Editor: content only
  • Technician: pairs and looks after screens
  • Safety officer: starts and clears emergency messages
  • Viewer: sees screens and logs
  • API tokens carry a role, never owner Pro
  • Roles at one site only, for the people who work there Enterprise
Who it suits

One organisation, many sites, or many customers

Organisations

A business, school or clinic with its own screens and no Home Assistant. Group screens by site, give each person a role, and keep a record of what was shown and who changed it.

Many sites

Sites can sit inside sites, as regions and branches do. Settings apply to one screen, a group, a site or the whole organisation. On Enterprise, people can hold a role at their own site only.

Partners, resellers and white label

Installers and resellers look after many customers from one sign-in, under their own name, colours, logo and domain, with their own help contact. Each customer decides whether its partner may reach its sites and screens.

Questions

Before you ask

When does the Console open?

We have not set a date. Ask for early access and we will tell you when it opens.

Do we need Home Assistant?

No. The Console runs screens by itself. If you have Home Assistant, Pro and Enterprise can connect to it, and its dashboards can go on Console screens too.

What do the screens need?

The PleatView app, on Google TV, Android TV, Fire TV, Android phones and tablets, or on Linux mini PCs and Raspberry Pi, which are in beta. Each screen connects out to the Console on port 443. No port is opened at the site.

What happens when a site loses its connection?

Screens play on, and keep to their schedule for weeks. Proof of play waits on the screen and is sent when the connection returns.

Can we try it before we pay?

Yes. When the Console opens, eachEach new organisation gets 30 days of Pro on up to 5 screens. You can also try the Console free on 2 screens.

What happens if a payment is late?

Your screens keep playing.

Can someone else look after our screens for us?

Yes. An installer or reseller can manage your organisation as your partner. Your owners decide whether the partner may reach your sites and screens, for the whole organisation or site by site, and every change is in your activity log.

Can we run the Console on our own server?

On request only. Choose Self-hosting in the form below and tell us where you would run it and why.

How is this different from PleatView with Home Assistant?

PleatView with Home Assistant is free and open source, runs on your own network and is driven by your automations. It stays free. The Console is a paid web app for organisations without Home Assistant. The comparison shows both, line by line.
