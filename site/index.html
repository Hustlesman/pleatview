Home Assistant, always on your TV.

PleatView turns a Google TV, Android TV or Fire TV into a display for your home, and a tablet, a mini PC or a Raspberry Pi too. Your dashboards stay on screen around the clock, and Home Assistant can put cameras, video and notifications on top.

What it does

Everything the big screen can do for your home

The TV joins Home Assistant as a media player and a notifier, ready for your automations.

Dashboards that stay up

Any dashboard, full screen, for weeks on end. PleatView signs in by itself, rotates between dashboards, and recovers from Wi‑Fi drops, Home Assistant restarts and browser crashes.

Sign in from your phone

A Grafana, NAS or router page asks for a password? The TV shows a code. Scan it, sign in on your phone with your password manager, and the TV remembers it, encrypted on the TV only.

Video in a corner

Cast from Home Assistant's media browser, full screen or picture-in-picture. Pop up a camera when the doorbell rings.

Notifications

notify.send_message puts a card on screen over whatever is on, with a picture or a camera snapshot if you like.

Any web dashboard

Add a Webpage dashboard in Home Assistant, for Grafana, UniFi or OpenSearch. The TV opens the site itself and zooms it to fit, so sites that refuse frames still work.

Announcements

tts.speak plays over whatever is on and turns it down meanwhile, so "the front door is open" interrupts the film, not the other way round.

Made for the remote

Channel up and down switch dashboards, Back closes pop-ups, and the menu needs no keyboard. Setup ends at a six-digit PIN, and PleatView speaks 11 languages.

Ideas

What people put on the big screen

Things Home Assistant users ask for on the forums, and the automation that does each with PleatView. Copy one, then change the entity names to yours.

A kitchen screen that turns into a photo frame

While someone's in the kitchen, it's the family board: photos, this week's calendar, the weather with tips for the washing and the lawn, and a QR code for the guest Wi-Fi. When nobody has been there for five minutes, an mmWave presence sensor switches it to just photos, like a screensaver.

A mini PC or a Raspberry Pi with a screen does it, and so do a tablet or a TV. No custom cards needed.

The automation
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.kitchen_presence
    to: "off"
    for: "00:05:00"
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.kitchen_presence
    to: "on"
actions:
  - action: pleatview.show_dashboard
    target:
      entity_id: media_player.kitchen_display
    data:
      path: >-
        {{ '/dashboard-kitchen' if trigger.to_state.state == 'on'
        else '/dashboard-photos' }}

Both dashboards and the weather tips, ready to copy

Who's at the door?

When the doorbell rings, the door camera opens in a corner of the TV and a card shows who's there. Over a film or another app, the card still appears.

The automation
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.doorbell
    to: "on"
actions:
  - action: pleatview.notify
    target:
      entity_id: media_player.living_room_tv
    data:
      title: Front door
      message: Someone is at the door.
      camera: camera.front_door
  - action: pleatview.show_camera
    target:
      entity_id: media_player.living_room_tv
    data:
      camera: camera.front_door
      position: bottom-left
      duration: 60

The washing machine is done

A smart plug sees the machine stop, and a card says so over whatever you're watching. Any automation can send one with notify.send_message.

The automation
triggers:
  - trigger: numeric_state
    entity_id: sensor.washing_machine_power
    below: 5
    for: "00:02:00"
actions:
  - action: notify.send_message
    target:
      entity_id: notify.living_room_tv
    data:
      title: Laundry
      message: The washing machine has finished.

A family board in the morning

Today's calendar, the weather and the shopping list from 7 to 9, then back to your usual dashboard. Custom cards work too: the TV runs Home Assistant's full frontend.

The automation
triggers:
  - trigger: time
    at: "07:00:00"
actions:
  - action: pleatview.show_dashboard
    target:
      entity_id: media_player.living_room_tv
    data:
      path: /dashboard-morning
      duration: 7200

A camera wall

Your cameras on a dashboard of their own, on a spare TV all day, or on the main one when there's motion outside at night.

The automation
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.driveway_motion
    to: "on"
conditions:
  - condition: sun
    after: sunset
actions:
  - action: pleatview.show_dashboard
    target:
      entity_id: media_player.living_room_tv
    data:
      path: /dashboard-cameras
      duration: 120

A wall of live streams

YouTube live streams on one TV, muted, all day: station cams, a city skyline, the space station, the news. One address holds them. For nine at once on a streaming stick, Home Assistant can render the wall and send the TV a single video of it.

The address and the steps

A wall tablet that wakes up for you

An Android tablet on the wall turns its screen on when someone walks up and off when they leave. Its battery sensor can also switch a smart plug, so it charges only between 20 and 80 %.

The automation
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.hallway_motion
    to: "on"
    id: "on"
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.hallway_motion
    to: "off"
    for: "00:05:00"
    id: "off"
actions:
  - action: media_player.turn_{{ trigger.id }}
    target:
      entity_id: media_player.hallway_tablet

Energy at a glance

Solar production by day and what each circuit uses at night, on a TV in the kitchen or office. Two automations at sunrise and sunset switch the dashboard.

The automation
triggers:
  - trigger: sun
    event: sunset
actions:
  - action: pleatview.show_dashboard
    target:
      entity_id: media_player.living_room_tv
    data:
      path: /dashboard-circuits
Set up

On your TV in three steps

No computer needed. It takes about five minutes.

Install the app

On the TV, install the free Downloader app from its app store, open it and enter this address. Android asks once to allow the install.

Downloaderpleatview.hustlesman.com/apk

Add the integration

On Home Assistant 2026.11 and later it comes from the built-in Marketplace, on older versions from HACS. Install it, then restart Home Assistant.

Open in Home Assistant

Press OK on the TV

Open PleatView on the TV. Home Assistant finds it under Settings → Devices & services: choose Add, then press OK on the TV to allow it. Or type the PIN from the screen.

Set up by address

The TV shows its PIN and a QR code. The code opens the TV's own page on your phone, which adds it to Home Assistant without typing.

Using the Marketplace? Until PleatView is in its catalogue, Home Assistant asks you to connect a GitHub account once before it adds PleatView from GitHub.

On a Raspberry Pi or a mini PC Beta

PleatView for Linux runs on a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 with the 64-bit Raspberry Pi OS, and on Ubuntu with an Intel or AMD processor. Open a terminal on it, or connect with SSH, and paste these two lines. They pick the right package for the computer.

wget -O /tmp/pleatview.deb https://github.com/Hustlesman/pleatview/releases/latest/download/pleatview_$(dpkg --print-architecture).deb
sudo apt install /tmp/pleatview.deb

To open PleatView whenever the computer starts, run pleatview-kiosk autostart on a desktop and restart, or sudo pleatview-kiosk enable without one (Raspberry Pi OS Lite, Ubuntu Server). Then add the integration and pair as above. The same two lines update it. The Linux guide has the details.

Tested on

Real devices, every Home Assistant release

Each release runs an automated test on a TV, a phone and a tablet against a real Home Assistant. It covers pairing, dashboards, kiosk mode, notifications, video, cameras, Webpage dashboards, sign-ins, standby, restarts, frozen pages and updates. The Linux app is checked by hand on a Raspberry Pi for now.

Open, local and yours

Open source

The app and the integration are on GitHub under the MIT license. GitHub Actions builds, signs and attests each release from its tagged code, so anyone can check where an APK came from.

Local only

No cloud service, no account and no tracking. The TV talks to your Home Assistant on your network, and to other sites only when your dashboards show them.

Sign-ins stay on the TV

Passwords for web dashboards are encrypted with the TV's keystore, offered only to their own site, and never sent to Home Assistant. Read the privacy policy.
