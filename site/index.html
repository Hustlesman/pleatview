Where it goes

One screen at home, or hundreds across your sites

The same apps run every screen. What changes is what you put on them.

Receptions and offices

Welcome visitors, show today's meetings and keep the team's numbers on the wall.

Shops and cafes

Menus, prices and offers that change with the time of day, at one shop or all of them.

Schools

Notices, timetables and events in every corridor, and one emergency message on every screen at once.

Clinics and waiting rooms

Opening hours, health notices and directions, kept up to date from the front desk.

Workshops and control rooms

Dashboards, cameras and live figures that stay up for weeks and recover by themselves.

Homes

The family calendar in the kitchen, the door camera on the TV and energy use at a glance.

PleatView Console Coming soon

Run every screen at every site from a browser

The Console is a web app for a computer, tablet or phone. It needs no Home Assistant. Screens connect out to it, so each site's network needs nothing opened.

Content library and playlists

Upload pictures, video, PDFs and presentations in the format you have. The Console converts them for each screen. Tag them and build playlists.

Layouts and a page builder

Split a screen into zones, or build a page with a clock, notices and feeds. Add buttons to make a touch page for a kiosk.

Schedules

Set what plays by hour, day and date, for one screen, a group or a whole site. Screens keep to it for weeks if the connection drops.

Emergency messages

One message over every screen in the organisation, or at the sites you choose, with a sound, until someone clears it.

Proof of play

A record of what each screen showed, when and for how long, for your reports or your advertisers.

Every screen at a glance

See what each screen shows now and whether it is online, and get an alert when one goes quiet.

Organisations, sites and roles

Each organisation's data is kept apart. Group screens by site, and give each person a role: owner, operator, editor, technician, safety officer or viewer.

Free with Home Assistant

Home Assistant, always on your TV

PleatView began here, and this part stays free and open source. The screen joins Home Assistant as a media player and a notifier, ready for your automations.

Home Assistant 2025.1 and later From the Marketplace or HACS

Dashboards that stay up

Any dashboard, full screen, for weeks on end. PleatView signs in by itself, rotates between dashboards, and recovers from Wi‑Fi drops, Home Assistant restarts and browser crashes.

Sign in from your phone

A Grafana, NAS or router page asks for a password? The TV shows a code. Scan it, sign in on your phone with your password manager, and the TV remembers it, encrypted on the TV only.

Video in a corner

Cast from Home Assistant's media browser, full screen or picture-in-picture. Pop up the door camera when the doorbell rings, even over a film in Plex or Netflix, which keeps playing with its sound.

Notifications

notify.send_message puts a card on screen over whatever is on, with a picture or a camera snapshot if you like. A warning adds a short chime while the film plays on; an alert pauses it and sounds an alarm.

Any web dashboard

Add a Webpage dashboard in Home Assistant, for Grafana, UniFi or OpenSearch. The TV opens the site itself and zooms it to fit, so sites that refuse frames still work. Steps in its address press buttons once it loads, found by their text or a CSS selector, to clear a cookie banner or close a pop-up, then drag a map or scroll.

Announcements

tts.speak plays over whatever is on and turns it down meanwhile, so "the front door is open" interrupts the film, not the other way round.

Made for the remote

Channel up and down switch dashboards, Back closes pop-ups, and the menu needs no keyboard. Setup ends at a six-digit PIN, and PleatView speaks 11 languages.

Ideas with Home Assistant

What people put on the big screen

Things Home Assistant users ask for on the forums, and the automation that does each with PleatView. Copy one, then change the entity names to yours.

A kitchen screen that turns into a photo frame

While someone's in the kitchen, it's the family board: photos, this week's calendar, the weather with tips for the washing and the lawn, and a QR code for the guest Wi-Fi. When nobody has been there for five minutes, an mmWave presence sensor switches it to just photos, like a screensaver.

A mini PC or a Raspberry Pi with a screen does it, and so do a tablet or a TV. No custom cards needed.

The automation
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.kitchen_presence
    to: "off"
    for: "00:05:00"
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.kitchen_presence
    to: "on"
actions:
  - action: pleatview.show_dashboard
    target:
      entity_id: media_player.kitchen_display
    data:
      path: >-
        {{ '/dashboard-kitchen' if trigger.to_state.state == 'on'
        else '/dashboard-photos' }}

Both dashboards and the weather tips, ready to copy

Who's at the door?

When the doorbell rings, the door camera opens in a corner of the TV and a card shows who's there. Over a film in Plex, Netflix or any other app, both show on top, live, while the film keeps playing with its sound.

The automation
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.doorbell
    to: "on"
actions:
  - action: pleatview.notify
    target:
      entity_id: media_player.living_room_tv
    data:
      title: Front door
      message: Someone is at the door.
      camera: camera.front_door
  - action: pleatview.show_camera
    target:
      entity_id: media_player.living_room_tv
    data:
      camera: camera.front_door
      position: bottom-left
      duration: 60

The washing machine is done

A smart plug sees the machine stop, and a card says so over whatever you're watching. Any automation can send one with notify.send_message.

The automation
triggers:
  - trigger: numeric_state
    entity_id: sensor.washing_machine_power
    below: 5
    for: "00:02:00"
actions:
  - action: notify.send_message
    target:
      entity_id: notify.living_room_tv
    data:
      title: Laundry
      message: The washing machine has finished.

A family board in the morning

Today's calendar, the weather and the shopping list from 7 to 9, then back to your usual dashboard. Custom cards work too: the TV runs Home Assistant's full frontend.

A camera wall

Your cameras on a dashboard of their own, on a spare TV all day, or on the main one when there's motion outside at night.

A wall of live streams

YouTube live streams on one TV, muted, all day: station cams, a city skyline, the space station, the news. One address holds them. For nine at once on a streaming stick, Home Assistant can render the wall and send the TV a single video of it.

The address and the steps

A wall tablet that wakes up for you

An Android tablet on the wall turns its screen on when someone walks up and off when they leave. Its battery sensor can also switch a smart plug, so it charges only between 20 and 80 %.

The automation
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.hallway_motion
    to: "on"
    id: "on"
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.hallway_motion
    to: "off"
    for: "00:05:00"
    id: "off"
actions:
  - action: media_player.turn_{{ trigger.id }}
    target:
      entity_id: media_player.hallway_tablet

Set up with Home Assistant

On your TV in three steps

No computer needed. It takes about five minutes.

Install the app

On Fire TV, from the Amazon Appstore: search for PleatView on the TV. On Google TV and Android TV, from Google Play's beta: join the testers' group, then the test.

No store? Install the free Downloader app, open it and enter pleatview.hustlesman.com/apk; Android asks once to allow the install.

Latest release v1.0.18, 12 November 2026. What's new

Add the integration

On Home Assistant 2026.11 and later it comes from the built-in Marketplace, on older versions from HACS. Install it, then restart Home Assistant.

Open in Home Assistant

Press OK on the TV

Open PleatView on the TV. Home Assistant finds it under Settings → Devices & services: choose Add, then press OK on the TV to allow it. Or type the PIN from the screen.

Set up by address

The TV shows its PIN and a QR code. The code opens the TV's own page on your phone, which adds it to Home Assistant without typing.

Using the Marketplace? Until PleatView is in its catalogue, Home Assistant asks you to connect a GitHub account once before it adds PleatView from GitHub.

On a Raspberry Pi or a mini PC Beta

PleatView for Linux runs on a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 with the 64-bit Raspberry Pi OS, and on Ubuntu with an Intel or AMD processor. Open a terminal on it, or connect with SSH, and paste these two lines. They pick the right package for the computer.

wget -O /tmp/pleatview.deb https://github.com/Hustlesman/pleatview/releases/latest/download/pleatview_$(dpkg --print-architecture).deb
sudo apt install /tmp/pleatview.deb

To open PleatView whenever the computer starts, run pleatview-kiosk autostart on a desktop and restart, or sudo pleatview-kiosk enable without one (Raspberry Pi OS Lite, Ubuntu Server). Then add the integration and pair as above. The same two lines update it. The Linux guide has the details.

Tested on

Real devices, every Home Assistant release

It covers pairing, dashboards, kiosk mode, notifications, video, cameras, Webpage dashboards, sign-ins, standby, restarts, frozen pages and updates. The Linux app gets the same kind of test in Docker, and is checked by hand on a Raspberry Pi.

Open, local and yours

Open source

The app and the integration are on GitHub under the MIT License. GitHub Actions builds, signs and attests each release from its tagged code, so anyone can check where an APK came from.

Local with Home Assistant

With Home Assistant there is no cloud service, no account and no tracking. The screen talks to your Home Assistant on your network, and to other sites only when your dashboards show them.

Sign-ins stay on the TV

With Home Assistant, passwords for web dashboards are encrypted with the TV's keystore, offered only to their own site, and never sent to Home Assistant. Read the privacy policy.
